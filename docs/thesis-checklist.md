# Thesis Checklist

Work through this list before you hand in your thesis.
For much more advice on organizing, conducting, writing, and presenting a thesis, read
[Student Thesis Projects](https://doi.org/10.3218/4241-2) by Lichter, Ludewig, Deininger, and
Schneider (open access).

## Readable from beginning to end

- [ ] Every term, concept, and acronym is introduced before it is used.
- [ ] References to earlier parts are fine; references to later parts are rare in the running text.
- [ ] Each chapter ends in a way that leads to the next one.

## Readable in ten minutes

A reader who opens the thesis anywhere and has ten minutes must find their way.

- [ ] No heading is directly followed by another heading: there is always text after a heading.
- [ ] Each chapter starts with an introductory paragraph that outlines its sections.
- [ ] This paragraph refers back to what the chapter builds on and forward to where its results are used.
- [ ] The introduction alone summarizes the whole argument: problem, method, results, and their interpretation.

## Scientific argument

- [ ] The research questions are stated precisely.
- [ ] The method is described so that someone else could reproduce your steps.
- [ ] Results are reported first and interpreted separately.
- [ ] The conclusion answers the research questions and names limitations and future work.
- [ ] The abstract and the Kurzzusammenfassung were written last and match the thesis.

## Sources

- [ ] Every statement that is not your own has a citation; direct quotes are marked as such.
- [ ] Bibliography entries are complete (authors, title, venue, year, DOI where available).
- [ ] Every cited web page states when you last visited it (`urldate` field).
- [ ] The data availability section states where the replication package (with DOI) is available.
- [ ] The use of AI tools is documented in the *Declaration of AI Use*, including the exact model and version for tools such as Claude Code, ChatGPT, or GitHub Copilot.

## Figures, tables, and listings

- [ ] Each one is referenced and discussed in the text.
- [ ] Each caption is understandable on its own.
- [ ] Text in figures is legible, also when printed.

## Form

- [ ] `mode = final` is set in `main.tex`, and `make check` reports no problems (no errors, no `??`, no overfull boxes, no TODO notes or template examples left).
- [ ] All hints and examples of this template are removed, including the appendix *LaTeX Examples*.
- [ ] Unused lists (e.g., the list of algorithms) are removed from `main.tex`.
- [ ] The text is spell-checked and grammar-checked, e.g., with LTeX+ in VS Code.
- [ ] The title page is correct: title in English and German, your name, course of study, examiner, supervisors, dates.
- [ ] The submitted PDF is signed as your examination office requires (e.g., digitally via the TUM Trust Center).
- [ ] A version you publish uses `signature = false` and contains no image of your handwritten signature.
- [ ] If required (e.g., for archiving or publication), the PDF is a valid PDF/A (see the README).
- [ ] The formal requirements of your examination regulations are met (page limit, file format, submission via the portal).
